---
title: For auditors
description: What a series of coverage records gives an auditor, what it does not, and how to pull every record from a registry on Stellar.
---

An auditor reviewing an anchor or a custodial app that publishes coverage can add its records to the evidence file and pull the full history from Stellar without access to the institution.

## What a record gives you

Each record is a dated result, checked on Stellar, that reserves were at least what the institution owed its customers at one ledger. Records arrive with each cycle, so between two audits you have a series. Cycles run on request, or daily or hourly when the orchestrator's scheduler is turned on.

| What you get                  | Why it helps                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One result per cycle          | Evidence for the days between your reports, not only the day you test.                                                                                     |
| A time from the network       | Each record carries the close time of the ledger that wrote it. The institution cannot backdate it.                                                        |
| A check you can repeat        | The registry checked the proof before writing the record. You can read every record yourself, with no access to the institution.                           |
| Visible gaps                  | A day with no record shows as a gap. It is never filled in later.                                                                                          |
| A commitment to the full list | The commitment root stands for the exact customer list and its total. Holders can check their own balance against it.                                      |
| A limit on window dressing    | Each record's reserves can be at most 10 percent above the record before, so funds moved in shortly before a cycle cannot lift reserves by more than that. |

## What a record does not give you

A record is evidence for your file, not an audit.

| A record does not                           | What still needs your procedures                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Test the institution's controls             | How the customer list is produced, who can change it, and how reserve accounts are run.                                         |
| Confirm custody                             | Who controls the reserve accounts, and whether the custodian holds what it signs for.                                           |
| Prove the reserve figure is honest          | The proof takes the custodian's signed balance as given. See [Where reserve figures come from](/docs/overview/reserve-sources). |
| Prove the customer list is complete         | The institution supplies the list. A left-out customer shows only when that customer checks.                                    |
| Show that reserves are free of other claims | A balance does not show if the same funds back something else.                                                                  |
| Say anything about a day with no record     | A missing day has no proof. It says nothing either way.                                                                         |

For how records sit beside audits and attestations, see [Coverage and audits](/docs/overview/coverage-and-audits).

## Pull the history of records

A registry numbers its records from 1, adding one each time, and `get_proof` returns the record with a given number. No call returns the count, so read from 1 upward until the registry answers error 5, `ProofNotFound`.

You need the Stellar CLI and a funded Testnet key named `reader` (see [Check a record on Stellar](/docs/verify/check-a-record-on-stellar#before-you-start)).

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Read every record

    This loop reads the Solstice Exchange example registry and stops at the first id with no record:

    ```bash
    REGISTRY=CDSQ35U2Y3VC44PJC6RRUJVCES2DLBWELDN32AIAEY4J35H4WBEKEUQW
    id=1
    while stellar contract invoke --id "$REGISTRY" --network testnet --source-account reader -- get_proof --id "$id" 2>/dev/null; do
      id=$((id + 1))
    done
    ```

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Read the output

    Each line is one record, oldest first:

    ```json
    {"l":"9000000","r":"16000000","root_h":"0c5c65b8b72f420da5a035470aa35ce6bce46a8309bd605cb10b9392d82c922e","timestamp":1783031356}
    {"l":"9000000","r":"16000000","root_h":"0c5c65b8b72f420da5a035470aa35ce6bce46a8309bd605cb10b9392d82c922e","timestamp":1793742470}
    {"l":"9000000","r":"16000000","root_h":"0c5c65b8b72f420da5a035470aa35ce6bce46a8309bd605cb10b9392d82c922e","timestamp":1793742635}
    {"l":"9000000","r":"16000000","root_h":"0c5c65b8b72f420da5a035470aa35ce6bce46a8309bd605cb10b9392d82c922e","timestamp":1793803080}
    ```

    `r` is reserves and `l` is what is owed, both in the asset's smallest unit. `timestamp` is the ledger close time in Unix seconds, UTC. For every field, see [Check a record on Stellar](/docs/verify/check-a-record-on-stellar#what-each-field-means).

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Line the records up with the calendar

    Convert each `timestamp` to a date and mark the days with no record. The demo registry above publishes on demand, not daily, so its records bunch together. A registry on a schedule publishes daily or hourly, and the gaps in its series are the missed cycles.

  </Step>
</Steps>

You now hold every record in the registry, read from Stellar. To see the transaction behind each record, look the contract up on a Stellar explorer, such as `https://stellar.expert/explorer/testnet/contract/<contract-id>`.

<Callout type="warn">
  The loop stops at the first read that fails. A record nobody has read for more than 30 days can be
  archived by the network, and its read fails until the entry is restored. If the loop stops earlier
  than you expect, try the next ids before you conclude the history ends there.
</Callout>
