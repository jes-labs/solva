title: Why it matters
description: The value a daily coverage record brings to institutions, partners and holders on Stellar.
---

A daily coverage record changes what partners and holders can see between audits, and it does so without exposing customers.

## Evidence between audits

An audit or attestation describes one date and arrives weeks later. Between reports, partners and holders rely on trust.

A Solva record adds evidence for every day in between. Each record is dated and tied to a Stellar ledger. An auditor sees a row for every day of the period, and a gap wherever a cycle did not run. The audit stays in place. See [Coverage and audits](/docs/overview/coverage-and-audits).

## Customer privacy

Publishing every balance behind a total would prove it, and would expose every customer.

Solva proves the total and publishes no balance. The public sees the reserves total, the total owed and a commitment, and each customer can check their own balance. See [Privacy](/docs/overview/privacy).

## A public record anyone can check

A record does not ask anyone to trust Solva. A contract on Stellar checks every proof before it writes a record, and the contract's code is open. Anyone can read a record and repeat the check, with no account and no permission.

Reading a record is free. Verifying a proof on Stellar Testnet costs about 0.0122514 XLM in network fees, as measured on the verifier contract `CA3KQYYAGUEIURJSB5CSWVMNIY2FF2EG3YTM7SBBRSN7DQWZXCCXWXN3`.

## One way to state coverage on Stellar

Each institution states its reserves in its own words and its own format, and partners read each one differently.

Solva gives every institution the same record, with the same fields and the same [statuses](/docs/overview/records), covered, short and stale. A wallet, an exchange or a lender reads every institution the same way. A contract can read it too, and `is_covered` <ComingSoon /> reduces that read to one call.
