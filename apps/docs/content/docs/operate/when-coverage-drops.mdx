---
title: When coverage drops
description: What happens when reserves fall below what is owed, how to respond, how to explain it to holders and partners, and the early warning signals.
---

When your reserves fall below what is owed, Solva publishes no new record. Follow these steps to find the cause, restore coverage and tell the people who rely on you.

## What happens

When the reserves total is below the total owed, no proof can be made:

1. The prover checks the totals first and refuses the cycle. The API answers `422` with `reserves are below liabilities; no proof generated`.
2. Nothing is published. A proof made some other way fails too, because the registry refuses any proof where reserves are below what is owed, with error 4, `InsolventBound`.
3. Your registry keeps its last record, with its older time.

Your status is [short](/docs/overview/records) for that cycle. If no new record arrives inside the expected window, daily by default, the record becomes [stale](/docs/overview/records). Readers see the time of your last record and can tell that no newer one exists.

A scheduled cycle that is refused is retried up to 3 times, then again at the next due run. It succeeds as soon as reserves cover what is owed again.

## Respond

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Confirm the result

    Check the `422` response or the orchestrator's logs. Then check the two totals yourself: the sum of the signed reserve figures, and the sum of the customer balances you loaded.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Find the cause

    Common causes are a reserve movement that has not settled, a timing gap between the custodian's statement and your balance export, a real rise in customer balances, or a data error such as a balance in the wrong unit or a row counted twice.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Fix the cause, not the data

    If reserves are short, restore them at the custodian. If the data is wrong, correct it to match your books. Never remove a customer or lower a balance to pass a cycle. A customer who was left out fails the inclusion check, and that failure is visible to them.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Run a cycle again

    Send `POST /v1/cycles` with a new idempotency key. A key that ran before is never run again. See [Run cycles](/docs/operate/run-cycles).

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Tell the people who rely on you

    Use the guidance below. Tell them early, before they find the gap on their own.

  </Step>
</Steps>

When reserves cover what is owed again, the next cycle returns `202` and your registry holds a new record with a current time.

<Callout type="warn">
  The growth bound applies on the way back. Reserves may rise at most 10 percent per cycle against
  your last record. If your last record was made at a lower reserves total, a large top-up can take
  several cycles to publish.
</Callout>

## Explain it to holders and partners

Say what happened, in plain words, with the times. A useful notice covers:

| Point                    | Example                                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What the record shows    | "Our coverage record has not been updated since 09:00 UTC on 4 November."                              |
| Why                      | "At the next check, our reserves were below the balances we owe customers, so no proof was published." |
| The size, if you know it | "The gap was USD 120,000, under 1 percent of balances."                                                |
| What you are doing       | "We are moving funds from our operating account to the reserve account."                               |
| When to expect a record  | "We expect a new record by 15:00 UTC."                                                                 |
| How to check             | "Read our record at the contract id below, and check your own balance was counted."                    |

Do not describe a short or stale day as covered. Partners read the record's time and can see that it has not moved.

## Early warning signals

The oracle tool `get_anomalies` compares each record with the one before it and flags changes that often come before a shortfall. It uses only the public totals and times, never a customer balance.

| Signal           | Kind               | Flags when                                                        | Critical at        |
| ---------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Reserve drift    | `reserve_drift`    | Reserves fall by 10 percent or more against the prior cycle.      | 20 percent or more |
| Liability spike  | `liability_spike`  | What is owed rises by 15 percent or more against the prior cycle. | 30 percent or more |
| Cycle timing gap | `cycle_timing_gap` | More than 26 hours pass between two records.                      | 52 hours or more   |

A flag at or past its threshold is a warning. A flag at twice the threshold or more is critical. Each flag names the record it was seen on and gives the measured value.

<Callout type="info">
  The oracle reads only your latest record, so it has no prior cycle to compare with and returns no
  flags.
</Callout>
